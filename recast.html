Recast

Listing prep for small-business brokers. Upload a seller's P&L, get the real earnings, the buyer's questions, and a first-draft CIM.

  • Python
  • Flask
  • LLM API
  • Vercel
See a sample report
Early. Live and being tested with Ontario business brokers. This page is the working doc.

The problem

When a small business is sold, the broker has to show buyers what it actually earns. The tax books rarely say: the owner's salary, depreciation, loan interest and one-off costs all push reported profit down. The broker adds those back to get seller's discretionary earnings (SDE), which is what the price is based on, then writes a long sales document called a CIM. It's hours of spreadsheet and writing work per listing.

How it works

  1. Upload. The broker uploads the seller's P&L (CSV or Excel) and a few notes.
  2. Recast. Every addback is matched to a line item and tagged "standard" or "needs documentation." The broker can change any of them and the numbers update live.
  3. Flag. It checks what a buyer will push on: falling revenue, margin swings, addback-heavy earnings, and an asking price outside the valuation range.
  4. Draft. An LLM writes the CIM sections, buyer questions and a document checklist from the computed figures. The broker edits and saves it as a PDF.

Under the hood

  • Flask app on Vercel. Uploads are parsed in memory and never stored.
  • All financial math is deterministic Python, so every number traces back to a line in the file. The model only writes prose.
  • The model is given pre-computed facts (multiples, range position, addbacks) and a post-filter removes claims that contradict the recast.

Business model

  • First report free on a live listing.
  • $149 per listing after that, or a monthly plan for brokers with several listings.
